# Tube of MPC corrections around a spine demonstration

Motivation (2026-10-05): the verified cold-reset demonstrations are chaotic as open-loop sequences. For seed_193, perturbing control 0 by L2 0.0003 and replaying the remaining recorded controls fails (state divergence appears after 9-10 controls at a contact event); L2 0.003 at any of controls 0-9 fails every trial, and a policy whose mean lies within 0.00-0.03 of the recorded controls at every spine state still fails from prefixes 4-9. The MPC succeeds because it re-plans from the true state every control. A policy therefore needs a feedback law, and the supervision for it is the MPC's correction at states near a successful path. `collect_tube.py` perturbs one control of the spine by a chosen radius and lets the archived MPC recipe complete the turn; every stored episode is a verified, bit-exactly replayable cold-reset trajectory whose rows after the perturbed control are correction labels at off-spine states (`control_origin`: `demo_prefix`, `perturbed`, `mpc`). The perturbed control itself is never a learning target. Nothing here is primitive-eligible.

## Planner choice (2026-10-05)

The first collection (`episodes/`, 64 verified branches, mostly at controls 10-13; `attempts_seed_193_*.jsonl`) used the archived re-planning CEM (warm start = hold the current target). Its corrections are not local: at states 0.06-0.26 from the spine (observation L2) the first MPC control lies 2.8-3.5 from the spine's own next control and 1.6-3.1 from the corrections in the other directions, i.e. the planner finds a different full solution each time, and such labels are multimodal (an actor regressed on them averages them; see `mpc_prefix6_handoff_recovery_0001` and the DAgger stage `bootstrap_variant_0080`). The second collection (`tracking/`, `tracking_cem.py`, `--tracking 0.1`) warm-starts every plan with the spine's own upcoming controls and scales the sampling floors by 0.1, so the planner searches locally and returns the reference whenever it is feasible and cheapest. Coherence test (`coherence_test/`, controls 4 and 7, radii .01/.03, 3 directions): 12/12 strict successes, first correction 0.2-0.6 from the spine control, 0.25-0.6 between directions, the whole correction segment 0.5-0.6 from the time-aligned spine controls, and the same number of controls to finish as the spine. These are the labels of a local feedback law around the spine.

## Second-order branches and DAgger (2026-10-06)

`collect_tube.py ... --tracking 0.1 --second-order tracking/episodes --offsets 1,3 --shard k/4 --out tracking2` perturbs a corrected control of a first-order branch (offset 1 or 3 after its perturbation; the parent is the tracking reference); `dagger_tube.py <agent.pt> <k_from> <k_to> 1,2,3,4 <out>` rolls a frozen learner out from spine prefixes and branches after 1-4 of its own controls (reference = the spine from the branch index). Review notes: the second-order shards partition the parent listing taken at launch, so a restart after the parent collection grew would recompute other shards' children (freeze the parent list before any resume); the perturbation-direction seed of the existing collections is not injective in (j, radius) (the same unit vector recurs at (j, .01), (j-2, .03) and (j-9, .1)); future collections use an injective SeedSequence. Second-order reports carry `parent`, `order`, `offset`; their `control_origin` marks every replayed parent row as `demo_prefix`, so learners must hold out by the root (first-order) direction.
