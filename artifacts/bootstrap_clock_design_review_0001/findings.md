# Design review: one explicit bootstrap clock is the smaller representation test

**Recommendation:** retain a bootstrap-only clock as a possible isolated representation diagnostic, but do not treat it as an established full-turn remedy or evidence of noise-tolerant SAC initialization. The now-completed paired experiment separates two problems: neural feedback amplifies noisy deviations into palm failures, while direct action noise still causes all five fixed-mean executions to miss a turn within the recorded nine controls. A clock can separate the demonstrated hold/move timing labels; it supplies neither proven physical noise tolerance nor the missing recovery commands. If that representation question is selected later for a bounded test, an artifact-local clock is cleaner than changing reset because it leaves every physical transition, reward, command mapping, and success/support criterion unchanged. No experiment was run or assigned in this review.

Broader actual corrective-feedback demonstrations, or a separately bounded fresh SAC experiment with realized-return and critic-calibration monitoring, address the missing adaptive-feedback evidence more directly than a clock-only change. Neither is established to work by these samples. Any such experiment needs a separate budget and truthful provenance; no experiment was run or assigned in this review.

## What the evidence supports

The first teacher command holds the reset target; the second starts motion. Their previous-action inputs are identical. Their 60-dimensional observations are distinct, but only 0.005414867 apart in L2 distance (maximum component 0.003089159), while target actions differ by 1.770271788 in L2 distance (maximum 0.881450314). This is a steep, schedule-dependent fitting demand, **not an exact duplicate-state contradiction**. The current reset returns zero finite-difference velocity slots; after the real first hold, the slots describe the small settling motion. Thus the actor must infer the hold-to-move phase from small physical differences.

The consistent-label actor already completes one deterministic strict turn. At existing minimum latent standard deviation 1e-5, the five prescribed noisy closed-loop runs all eventually incur palm support, and one also drops. Root's reported step-two conditional-mean drift of 14–256 times direct sampling noise supports a feedback-amplification hypothesis. The paired fixed-mean experiment now verifies the same standardized Gaussian draws and exact first action/post-step state. At command2, feedback-versus-fixed action gaps are 0.000193–0.004213, while the largest direct normalized-action noise over the fixed sequences is about3.0592e-5.

All five fixed-mean runs remain grasped without palm/drop/external support at the common nine-control cutoff, but their final force-qualified angles are only3.684,5.647,4.560,5.413,4.525rad. None reaches2π. This supports both feedback amplification and remaining direct physical progress sensitivity. Four feedback palm failures occur within the same nine-control window; the fifth starts at0.904s in control10, beyond the fixed-mean experiment's cutoff. Fixed-mean survival past0.9s is untested. Removing learned state feedback therefore does not recover the deterministic first-turn behavior within the recorded duration. It also does not prove that a better clock-conditioned feedback controller could never recover: those five retained partial states have no demonstrated continuation/recovery labels in the current data.

Appending a seconds-valued clock differing by 0.1 between those startup observations raises their numerical coordinate distance to 0.100146497 and reduces their action-distance ratio from 326.928 to 17.6768. Those mixed-unit distances document the representation change, not a physical metric or a prediction of learned Jacobian reduction. Clock conditioning permits a different function with less dependence on those small state differences; optimization may still learn a sensitive function.

The cached companion paper describes primitive extraction from a simulation SAC policy on the matched 40 mm object without domain randomization. That passage does not specify a bootstrap elapsed-time input. A clock would therefore be an explicit local bootstrap choice, not an author-provided recipe. No current BC-plus-head-refinement result is a SAC result.

## Smallest isolated representation proof, if selected later

1. Keep the existing twelve actual pairs, their labels, and all frozen physical sources. Append only `float32(control_index * 0.1)` to each decision observation: nominal rows 1–9 receive 0.0–0.8 seconds, and the three actual recovery corrections receive 0.6, 0.7, 0.8. Do not restart the clock at the correction branch or fabricate a thirteenth action. The two branches have the same episode-time origin.
2. Use a local policy adapter that reads the real environment's completed-control count and appends `env.steps * control_seconds` immediately before actor inference. Leave the base environment and its 60-vector unchanged. The clock starts at zero only on a real episode reset, is preserved across resume/branch execution, and never wraps or resets at a full turn. Store both base60 and actual policy61 inputs plus the corresponding clock values. A recorded-policy control run through the adapter must reproduce the existing physics bitwise; this is an interface check, not another planner experiment.
3. Run one fresh 61/16 actor fit with the established 256-by-256 architecture, seed17001, mean-only BC12k, lr0.003, batch256, epsilon0.02, and the same CUDA singleton inference/runtime. Record fit metrics at3k/6k/12k; no hyperparameter or clock-scale search. Prespecify the previous numerical-precision rule: if final exact action-fit maximum exceeds1e-6, permit only one full-row-rank frozen-feature float64 residual solve to the same float32 atanh targets, reporting condition/correction magnitude. Preserve the unmodified BC checkpoint. This tests feasibility, not a statistically isolated estimate of the causal effect of adding one input.
4. Audit the selected final actor once under unchanged strict physics, then the same five fixed latent-noise seeds at the existing1e-5 floor, coupled to the paired experiment's noise draws where available. Preserve conditional means, sampled perturbations, actual actions, and full force/contact traces. Measure the step-two mean drift at a fixed clock separately from the direct sampling term. Any qualified clone also needs a separately recorded two-second continuation with the **actual policy**; a held command is not equivalent. First-turn success and post-success retention remain separate reported outcomes.

The useful evidence would be a fitted clock-conditioned actor with reduced measured state-dependent drift and better outcomes under the same prescribed perturbations. Given the paired result, reduced palm incidence alone would show only mitigation of the feedback pathology; qualified full turns would still require separate evidence. A deterministic replay, a smaller training loss, or a favorable feature condition number alone would not establish robust exploration. Five runs are a diagnostic sample, not a success-rate estimate. The dataset contains no post-success policy labels, so retention after0.9s remains an independent coverage limitation even if startup improves. This paragraph specifies a possible bounded test; no experiment was run or assigned in this review, and further BC is not established as sufficient.

The new interface must carry a distinct bootstrap-only kind, dimension61, clock origin/units/index, action mapping, and source hash. The existing main rotation interface remains60; existing61-dimensional reorientation means a goal angle, not time. Current main CLI/actor-import dimension gates should continue rejecting the new kind. No existing checkpoint metadata should be relabeled to bypass those gates. Any later bootstrap-SAC integration is separate work with truthful update provenance; eventual joint trajectories do not by themselves become eligible primitives.

## Actual0.1s reset settling is a different, more invasive test

Executing the first hold outside the learned policy could remove its hold/move label pair while retaining the physical benefit of settling. It is different from omitting that hold and moving directly from the cold reset; the latter branch's near-miss does not refute actual warmup. The hold must be physically executed and recorded, not replaced by teleporting to a saved settled state or zeroing velocity.

The simplest auditable version would retain the original cold-reset episode origin, execute the exact recorded hold, then hand the returned real60-vector to the actor at step1. That preserves existing angle/support history and finite-difference observations, but introduces an explicit scripted reset prelude: learned-control time, total elapsed time, and rotation credit must be reported separately. It cannot be presented as the original cold-reset policy acting from its first control.

If the task episode is instead rebased after settling, the design must consistently define initial position/orientation, axial-angle references, raw/retained/previous progress, prior action, finite-difference history, contact-loss accumulation, palm violation history, rotation/dwell counters, reward and horizon accounting, and simulator versus episode clocks. In particular, reinitializing finite-difference velocities to zero discards the measured settling observation; clearing palm/contact failure history can hide an invalid reset. The independent observer must start from the chosen frame while retaining a separate audit of all prelude physics and rejecting failed settling. Resume and evaluation must not execute the prelude twice. These are lifecycle and measurement changes beyond adding an observable clock.

For the current question, the clock adapter is the smaller controlled test. Neither option supplies evidence that direct noisy contact commands are safe, nor a robust feedback law for unseen contact states. No implementation, training, new physical rollout, lower noise bound, or SAC claim was made in this review.
